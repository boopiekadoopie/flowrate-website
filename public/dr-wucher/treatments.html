Check-up and GBT clean

Guided Biofilm Therapy (GBT) is a thorough, gentle cleaning protocol. Warm water, air and a fine powder lift the biofilm from your teeth and gums, and a slim ultrasonic tip removes hard tartar only where it's needed.

45 min8 steps
Certified practice Concept image

Who it suits

Most patients, as part of a regular check-up. It also suits implants, crowns and braces.

Step by step

  1. AssessWe check your teeth, gums and any implants.
  2. DiscloseA harmless dye colours the biofilm, so you can see where it sits.
  3. ExplainWe show you the coloured areas and how to reach them at home.
  4. AIRFLOWWarm water, air and a fine powder lift biofilm and light stains.
  5. PERIOFLOWA fine nozzle cleans deeper pockets, where you have them.
  6. PIEZONA slim ultrasonic tip removes any tartar that's left.
  7. CheckThe dentist makes a final check, including for decay.
  8. RecallWe agree when you should come back, based on what we found.

Materials

  • AIRFLOW: erythritol-based powder, warm water, air
  • PERIOFLOW nozzle
  • PIEZON ultrasonic tip

Equipment and protocol by EMS, the Swiss maker of GBT.

Limits and alternatives

GBT is a cleaning protocol. Studies find air-polishing with erythritol powder safe, with results similar to other non-surgical cleaning. If we find deeper gum problems, we'll explain the options, starting with gum treatment.

Questions

Will it be uncomfortable? +

Most people find it comfortable. Tell us if an area is sensitive, and we'll adjust or numb it.

How often should I come? +

At the end, we agree a recall time with you, based on what we found. Some people need a clean every six months, others more or less often.

Sources: the eight GBT steps (BDJ In Practice, 2024) · air-polishing review (Medicina, 2022)

Safe amalgam removal

Taking out old amalgam (mercury) fillings with protective steps, and restoring each tooth with a metal-free material.

Consultation firstPlanned with you

Protective steps Example, to confirm

  1. A dental damisolates the tooth
  2. High-volume suctionright at the tooth
  3. Air filtrationclose to your mouth
  4. Removed in pieces,not ground away
  5. A thorough rinseafterwards

Not during pregnancy or breastfeeding. Based on the IAOMT's SMART checklist; the practice's own steps go here.

Who it suits

People who've decided, with us, to replace old amalgam fillings: for example a filling that's cracked, leaking or worn, or because you'd prefer metal-free materials.

Fillings in good condition don't have to come out. Removing them can briefly raise exposure to mercury vapour and costs some healthy tooth. We'll look at yours, explain what we see, and you decide.

Step by step

  1. PlanWe check each filling and agree the order and the number of visits.
  2. IsolateA non-latex dental dam isolates the tooth, with suction under it.
  3. CaptureHigh-volume suction at the tooth, and air filtration close to your mouth.
  4. CoolPlenty of water keeps the filling cool while it's cut.
  5. SectionThe filling is cut into pieces and lifted out, not ground away.
  6. RinseYour mouth is rinsed thoroughly afterwards.
  7. RestoreThe tooth is rebuilt with a metal-free material.

Based on the IAOMT's SMART recommendations. The practice's own steps go here

Materials

  • Non-latex dental dam
  • High-volume suction
  • Air filtration at the source
  • Composite resin
  • Glass ionomer
  • Ceramic inlays, onlays and crowns

Limits and alternatives

Removal can't be undone, and each replacement takes a little more tooth. Leaving a sound filling in place is a reasonable choice, and we'll keep checking it at your check-ups.

Who should wait

We don't remove amalgam during pregnancy or while breastfeeding.

The wider picture

The practice hasn't placed amalgam in about 30 years, and the rules have moved the same way. In 2020 the US FDA advised some higher-risk groups to avoid new amalgam fillings where possible. The European Union banned dental amalgam from 1 January 2025. Under the Minamata Convention, which Namibia joined in 2017, countries agreed in 2025 to phase out dental amalgam by 2034.

Questions

Will removing my fillings make me feel better? +

We can't promise that, and we won't. We replace amalgam when a filling needs replacing, or because you'd prefer metal-free materials, and we do it with protective steps. If you have health concerns, we'll talk them through alongside your own doctor.

How many visits will it take? +

That depends on how many fillings you have and where they are. Your written plan shows the visits, in the order we'd do them.

Sources: US FDA, 24 September 2020 · European Commission, 2024 · Minamata Convention amendments · IAOMT SMART checklist

Zirconia and titanium implants

An implant replaces the root of a missing tooth and carries a crown, a bridge or a full arch. You choose between zirconia, a white ceramic, and titanium, a metal.

Consultation firstOne tooth to a full arch
ZirconiaWhite ceramic. A metal-free option, with good results so far.Concept image

Who it suits

Adults with one or more missing teeth, and enough healthy bone, or bone that can be built up first.

Step by step

  1. ConsultationWe look at your mouth, gums and X-rays, and talk through the materials.
  2. PlanScans and a written plan in three levels, with the visits and a quote.
  3. Healthy gums firstAny gum treatment comes before the implant.
  4. PlacementThe implant is placed under local anaesthetic.
  5. HealingUsually a few months, while the implant joins the bone.
  6. Your new toothThe crown, bridge or arch is fitted.
  7. Looking after itRegular check-ups and GBT cleans, which suit implants.

Materials

  • Zirconia implants, one-piece or two-piece
  • Titanium implants
  • Ceramic crowns

The implant systems the practice uses: to confirm

Limits and alternatives

Zirconia is a metal-free option with good results so far. Titanium has the longest track record. In studies, two-piece zirconia implants have done less well than one-piece ones. A bridge or a removable denture can also replace a tooth, and we'll talk these through too.

The evidence

In a 2023 review of zirconia implants (25 studies, 4,017 implants), 95.1% were still in place after 10 years. A 2023 meta-analysis found similar survival for zirconia and titanium at 12 months, and called for longer trials.

Who should wait

Gum disease is treated first. We'll ask about your health, smoking and medicines, because some need planning before an implant.

Questions

Is zirconia better than titanium? +

Neither is better for everyone. Zirconia is metal-free and white; titanium has the longest track record. We'll look at your mouth and talk through which suits you.

Can implants replace a full arch? +

Yes. Implants can carry anything from one crown to a full arch of teeth. Dr Andreae's work includes implants and full-mouth rehabilitation.

Sources: zirconia implant review (Mohseni et al., 2023) · zirconia and titanium meta-analysis (Clinical Oral Investigations, 2023)

Fillings and crowns

Repairing a tooth with metal-free materials: tooth-coloured composite, glass ionomer or ceramic.

Metal-free materialsUsually one visit

Metal-free materials What we use

  • Composite resinTooth-coloured, bonded in thin layers.Most fillings
  • Glass ionomerBonds chemically to the tooth.Near the gum line, and children's teeth
  • CeramicHard-wearing, made to fit the tooth.Inlays, onlays and crowns

Who it suits

Anyone with a cavity, a cracked or worn tooth, or an old filling that needs replacing.

Step by step

  1. CheckWe look at the tooth, with an X-ray if it's needed.
  2. ChooseWe explain your options and the material we'd suggest.
  3. ComfortLocal anaesthetic, if you need it.
  4. Clean outDecay or the old filling is removed.
  5. RestoreMost fillings take one visit; ceramic inlays, onlays and crowns may take two.
  6. FinishWe check your bite and polish the surface.

Materials

  • Composite resin
  • Glass ionomer cement
  • Ceramics

The US FDA names composite resin and glass ionomer cement as non-mercury options.

Limits and alternatives

Every filling material wears over time. A large filling may need an inlay or a crown to protect the tooth, and we'll tell you when that's the better route.

Questions

Can I have a white filling instead of a silver one? +

Yes. The practice has been amalgam-free for about 30 years, so new fillings are tooth-coloured.

How long will a filling last? +

It depends on its size, where it is and how you chew. We check every filling at your check-ups.

Source: US FDA, 24 September 2020

Gum treatment

Treating inflamed gums and the pockets between gum and tooth, so your teeth and implants have healthy support.

60 minHealthy gums first

4 mm or more: we take a closer look

Who it suits

Anyone whose gums bleed when they brush, feel tender or have pulled back, and anyone with implants.

Step by step

  1. Gum checkWe measure the pockets around each tooth, like the example.
  2. Deep cleanGBT, and careful cleaning below the gum line, with local anaesthetic if needed.
  3. At homeWe show you what to change at home, and why.
  4. Re-checkWe measure again after a few weeks.
  5. Keep it upRegular cleans, at an interval we agree with you.

Materials

  • Guided Biofilm Therapy: AIRFLOW, PERIOFLOW, PIEZON
  • Hand instruments where needed

Limits and alternatives

Bone that's been lost around a tooth doesn't grow back on its own. Treatment aims to stop gum disease getting worse. Some advanced cases need surgery, and Dr Andreae's work includes oral surgery.

Questions

Why do my gums matter for my implants? +

Implants need healthy gum and bone around them, just like natural teeth. That's why gum health comes first in every plan.

Will the deep clean hurt? +

We use local anaesthetic where it helps, and you can ask us to pause at any time.

Source: the eight GBT steps (BDJ In Practice, 2024)

Orthodontics

Moving teeth into a better position with clear aligners or braces, planned around your whole face, jaw and bite.

Consultation firstChildren and adults

Your options Example

  1. ConsultationTeeth, bite, jaw and face
  2. RecordsPhotos, scans and X-rays
  3. Your planOptions, timing and a written quote
  4. Aligners or bracesWith regular check-ins
  5. RetainerKeeps your teeth where they are

Who it suits

Children, teenagers and adults with crowded or crooked teeth, gaps, or a bite that doesn't meet comfortably.

Step by step

  1. ConsultationWe look at your teeth, bite, jaw and face.
  2. RecordsPhotos, scans and X-rays.
  3. Your planYour options, how long each takes, and a written quote.
  4. TreatmentClear aligners or braces, with regular check-ins.
  5. RetainerA retainer keeps your teeth in their new place.

Materials

  • Clear aligners
  • Tooth-coloured or metal braces
  • Fixed or removable retainers

Which systems the practice uses: to confirm

Limits and alternatives

Teeth tend to move back without a retainer, so a retainer is part of every plan. Some bites also need jaw surgery, and we'll tell you if yours might.

Questions

Am I too old for orthodontics? +

No. Adults can have orthodontic treatment too, as long as their gums and bone are healthy.

Do you look at the jaw and face, not just the teeth? +

Yes. Planning looks at your bite, jaw and face, not only at how straight your teeth are.

Functional medicine consultation

A consultation with Dr Wucher about nutrition and lifestyle, using nutritional therapy and orthomolecular medicine, alongside your dental care or on its own.

60 minWith Dr Wucher
Concept image

Who it suits

Adults and families who want to understand how nutrition, sleep, stress and surroundings fit together with their dental care.

It works alongside your own doctor, never instead of them.

Step by step

  1. BeforeA short questionnaire about your health history, diet, sleep and medicines.
  2. The conversationDr Wucher asks about your diet, sleep, stress, energy, surroundings and dental history.
  3. Tests, if usefulHe may suggest tests that your doctor or a laboratory can arrange.
  4. Your planA written plan: nutrition, lifestyle and, where it helps, supplements.
  5. Your doctorYour own doctor stays in charge of your medical care.
  6. Follow-upWe meet again to see how the plan is going.

Process to confirm with Dr Wucher

Methods

  • Nutritional therapy
  • Orthomolecular medicine

Orthomolecular medicine plans vitamins, minerals and other nutrients in the amounts that suit you.

Limits

This consultation doesn't diagnose illness or replace medical treatment. If something needs your doctor, we'll say so.

Questions

Do I need to be a dental patient? +

No. You can book a functional medicine consultation on its own.

How does this fit with my own doctor? +

Your doctor stays in charge of your medical care. With your permission, we share your plan, so everyone works from the same picture.
